# Fluid tank integration safety — DO NOT ENABLE

This is an unregistered prototype, not a usable block or item. An experimental `FluidTankBlock` exists and creates a `FluidTankTile`, but neither is integrated for gameplay. The block unconditionally refuses the normal Forge player-removal callback, including creative and empty tanks; its vanilla piston reaction is `BLOCK`. It reports maximum explosion resistance, disables explosion drops and retains the block in the Forge explosion callback. These are limited defenses, not a universal guarantee: a block hook cannot prevent arbitrary `World.setBlockState`, commands, bypassing mods or automation, unload failures, crashes, or other removal/replacement routes; events or tool callbacks may also occur before player removal is refused. `mayRemoveSafely()` is only an advisory check on the server thread, not a security boundary or a way to remove the block. An empty/quarantined/filled tile can still be discarded by the world; no fluid-preserving item/drop recovery or persistence protocol is implemented. Never assume that blocking normal mining protects fluid or unknown NBT. Do not register, place, publish, or make persisted tank items from this prototype until all supported paths have an independently reviewed, in-world tested protection/recovery scheme.

`SafeFluidTank` owns one fluid, up to 16000 mB; simulations do not mutate it; unknown or malformed NBT (including a missing tag) is quarantined and the original raw tag retained. Fluid and tag copies do not intentionally share mutable ownership. The tile exposes its fluid capability only on the Minecraft server thread, for the live loaded chunk and matching tile. Previously obtained handles also check liveness on every fluid operation. Chunk unload/reload and multiplayer have not been exercised in an actual server.

A newly constructed tank starts empty and not quarantined. If Forge or another path silently recreates a missing tile without reading the original NBT, the replacement can therefore appear to be a valid empty tank, even though earlier fluid or unknown data may have been lost. Do not treat the empty view or `mayRemoveSafely()` as proof of a genuine new placement, safe removal, or recovery. `shouldRefresh` retains the existing tile for changes to the same block but allows refresh when the block changes; this does not protect against tile recreation, replacement, or crashes.

`read` is an **initial-load-only** operation. A second read, including one after a successful fluid mutation or quarantined load, throws rather than destroying existing ownership. The tile performs this check before `super.readFromNBT`. Integration must investigate Forge lifecycle/update packets, including inherited `getUpdateTag`/`handleUpdateTag`, and any mods that deserialize into an existing tile: such uses may throw and must be redesigned before enabling. The tank guards reentrant handler operations and serialization, but reentrancy through attached capabilities and superclass callbacks still needs review and testing; do not infer a general reentrancy guarantee. No migration, repair, item serialization, or recovery API is provided. Raw-tag preservation does not protect the tank from tile/block deletion.

`markDirty` is not an atomic durable write. A successful dirty callback or fluid transfer does not prove chunks, neighbor inventories, or items are saved atomically; crash/power loss, forced unload, disk-full, or failures after commit can lose or duplicate data. Post-commit RuntimeExceptions from dirty notification are logged while the committed operation remains successful; more severe failures and other mods remain outside this API. Isolated Java/Forge tests of removal callbacks and same-block tile preservation are not in-game, full-build, persistence or crash-recovery proofs. Backups and explicit crash-consistency design/tests are prerequisites, not guarantees provided here.
